# HR Analytics & Employee Attrition Study

### Project Overview
This project uses MySQL to clean and analyze 22,214 employee records to determine which departments and groups lose people fastest. 

**Attribution:** Dataset is a public HR dataset used in many SQL tutorials. Queries 1–11 follow the common question list; Query 12 (HQ vs Remote) and Query 13 (Tenure band retention), as well as the CTE-based hiring/termination joins, are my original extensions.

### Technical Stack & Methods
* **Database:** MySQL
* **SQL Methods:** CTEs, LEFT JOINs, CASE statements, Date/Time aggregations, and GROUP BY.

### Key Findings
* **Overall Baseline:** The company termination rate is **13.1%** (2,905 terminations).
* **Departmental Turnover:** Auditing shows the highest turnover at **19.2%**, though this is a small sample (52 employees). Legal is the sturdier headline with a **16.7%** turnover rate across 311 people.
* **Remote vs. Headquarters:** Headquarters employees have a turnover rate of 13.3%, while Remote employees are slightly lower at 12.3% (a 1-point gap, borderline at p≈0.05, so directional only).
* **Company Growth:** Hiring essentially stopped after 2020, while exits continued every year, meaning the company is actively shrinking.

### Data Quality Quirks 
* **Future Terminations:** 1,024 termination dates fall after today (up to 2041). They may be scheduled exits or entry errors, so I counted them as active in the queries.
* **Birthdate Errors:** 967 birthdates had invalid two-digit years (read as future years by MySQL resulting in negative ages) and were excluded from the age analysis.
